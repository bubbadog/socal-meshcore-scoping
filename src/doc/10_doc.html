01 · Summary

The short version

SoCal's mesh is busy enough that its own maintainer says it is starting to hit the range where messages get lost. This document proposes an order of work that reduces that load, and it lists what is still unknown.

about 2,850channel messages a day last week, roughly double the Q2 average of 1,332
68%of decodable channel messages are test, bot or wardriving traffic
countyis the level the maintainers' survey landed on: about 30 responses found city level too granular
0 of 30audited repeaters declare a named region today

Sources and caveats for each figure are in Why now and Names.

What this suggests

  1. Start with settings that need no agreement. The WCMesh repeater settings, advert limits, a hop-limit trial and quieter bots. Rollout, step 1.
  2. Use the maintainers' names, and propose none. The SoCal administrator's tree (us, west, california, socal, then counties, with local areas as optional tags) is already in the config generator, and his survey landed at county level. This edition withdraws the airport-code names of its working drafts. Names.
  3. Tag repeaters first, and leave their default scope alone until neighbors are tagged too. Otherwise the first repeater to move stops being heard beyond one hop. How scoping works.
  4. Scope machines before people. One MeshMapper setting and a few bot owners control most of the channel traffic. Channels.
  5. Measure airtime and delivery at every step, and stop when a step does not help. Measures.

What has to happen before the research is complete

  1. The Discord record was read on 13 October, by search, through the author's own account; a member's full read of the coordination thread would still help. The findings are folded in below and cited by message link.
  2. The SoCal administrator confirms the county-level names, which he has said the next State of the Mesh may cover.
  3. Airtime is measured at several repeaters, so the load picture rests on more than one site.
  4. Two pilots run: one to prove the mechanism, one to show whether load falls.
  5. People who know the network check this page. Every claim has a source so that it can be checked.

02 · A note from the author

Why I wrote this, and what I do not know

I am new to this technology. I have my repeater, SkyNet, set up in Thousand Oaks, and I am still learning how the network works.

I put this together because I would like the mesh to be useful, robust and efficient for everyone, and I think a little planning can help with that. I hope it can be a small contribution to a project that a lot of people have already put real work into.

I am not trying to step on anyone's toes. The people who built and run this network know far more about it than I do, and some of what follows is probably wrong or already decided somewhere I have not looked. If so, please tell me. That is what the feedback section and the open repository are for.

The SkyNet operator · GitHub: bubbadog

04 · Why now

The mesh is close to the load its maintainer warned about

[[F]] The Q2 2026 State of the Mesh says: "We are starting to hit that 20%-25% threshold before the mesh starts getting overwhelmed." [[@wc-q2:L39]] [[F]] The Q1 report defines the quantity: "Around 20% airtime utilization is the limit of when packets start to break down." [[@wc-q1:L22]] [[C]] The analyzer counted about 2,850 channel messages a day in the week to 13 October, against a Q2 average of 1,332. [[@cs-rf; wc-q2:L37]] [[U]] The two counts may not use the same method, so read that as "roughly double".

[[E]] At one repeater in the Conejo Valley, the radio's own counters for a 46-minute window on the afternoon of 10 October imply the channel was occupied about 22 percent of the time. [[@own-telemetry]] That is one site at a busy hour, and the all-day figure there is probably nearer 14 percent.

[[F]] Members describe the same picture from the other side. In one week in March the SoCal administrator counted 1,113 Public messages against 1,927 on the test and bot channels. [[@dc32]] One operator reports that the busiest SoCal repeaters stay under 10 percent duty cycle; that is transmit time only, not channel occupancy, so it does not contradict the 22 percent above. [[@dc51]] Another cites Seattle's experience that 18 to 20 percent is congested, 20 to 25 disruptive and 27 to 30 redline. [[@dc29]] A third wrote on 5 October that floods from high backbone sites are "becoming an issue worth problem solving" in the dense parts of the Inland Empire, Orange County, San Diego and Los Angeles. [[@dc31]] [[F]] In February a member measured adverts at 32.1 percent of airtime, before the 48-hour advert interval was pinned as the SoCal setting. [[@dc27; dc28]]

[[E]] Channel messages are 44.5 percent of relay airtime and 68.4 percent of them are test, bot or wardriving traffic, so that traffic is about 30 percent of all relay airtime, if airtime per message is similar across channels. Confining it to a metro that holds 30 percent of the repeaters would save roughly a fifth of all relay airtime. [[E]] County and city channels are about 1.1 percent of relay airtime on the same assumption. That is the most that scoping those channels could save.

05 · How scoping works

Regions live on repeaters. Scopes travel with floods.

[[F]] A region is a name in a repeater's table. A scope is a 16-bit code a sender puts on a flood packet, computed from a region name and the packet's own bytes. A repeater forwards a scoped flood only when the code matches a name it holds with flooding allowed. Each name is tested independently and a parent is never consulted. [[@fw-regionmap:L190-204; fw-tks:L4-18]] [[F]] A flood with no code matches the wildcard *, which every repeater allows out of the box. Packets sent along a known path are not scope-checked. [[@fw-regionmap:L46-49; fw-rep:L434-443, L556-568]]

The trap for whoever goes first

[[F]] A repeater sends its own flood adverts under its default scope. [[@fw-rep:L1031-1038, L1294-1297]] [[I]] So the first repeater in a neighborhood to set a default scope has its adverts dropped by every neighbor that lacks the name. [[F]] The SoCal air shows the pattern: three scoped adverts from one Coachella Valley repeater were each recorded after a single relay and no further, while an unscoped advert from SkyNet was recorded 64 times over paths up to 11 hops. [[@cs-packets; own-decode; cs-advert]] [[I]] The one repeater that relayed them either holds psp too or runs firmware too old to filter. Either way the adverts stopped where the name stopped. [[F]] A long-time operator's list of gotchas in the Discord says the same in fewer words: region default is "a loaded gun"; turned on early, it scopes all flood traffic, adverts included, and you "silently disappear". [[@dc13]]

Two more facts that shape a rollout

  • [[F]] A companion's replies go out under the replier's own scope setting, so a scoped user and an unscoped user can talk only while both kinds of flood are forwarded along the whole path. [[@fw-chat:L252-302; fw-comp:L500-510; ca-onqc:L768-769]]
  • [[F]] Names are case-sensitive on repeaters, and the published rule is lower case, at most 29 bytes, letters, digits and hyphen. [[@fw-regionmap:L52-55, L207-216; blog-region]]

06 · Names

The maintainers have chosen a direction. This plan follows it.

[[F]] The WCMesh docs carried a SoCal region list for about 22 hours in March 2026, inside a regions section that lasted about 55 hours. [[@wc-regions-old:L7-28; wc-log]] [[F]] The account that owns the WCMesh docs committed a second list, with counties and 52 local areas, to the matkam config generator on 3 and 24 August 2026. [[@mk-c1; mk-c2; mk-data:L112-118, L945]] [[F]] MeshMapper divides SoCal into five zones with airport codes. [[@mm-socal]] [[F]] Working drafts 3 to 5 of this plan proposed a fourth list, now withdrawn. [[@plan5:§05]]

[[F]] The Discord record shows where the maintainers are going. In the coordination thread the SoCal administrator laid out a tree of west, then california, then region (SoCal, Central Coast, Central Valley, Bay), then county, then area. [[@dc9]] The generator's maintainer wanted west at the top for compatibility with Cascadia; the administrator preferred us above it, and on 26 August his pull request adding us was approved. [[@dc10; dc11; dc12; dc40]] He argued against three-letter codes so that region names are not confused with airport and observer codes. [[@dc14]] A SoCal Zoom and a survey in August drew about 30 responses: people liked the structure and found city level too granular ("Counties seem good, plus certain tags"). [[@dc7; dc8]] [[F]] As of 13 October nothing is set. The administrator said the same in May, in September and again in October: "No regions have been set yet" (2 October), companions "won't be scoped any time soon" (20 September), and on the evening of 12 October: "We have been trying to map regions", with the next State of the Mesh possibly covering regions. [[@dc1; dc3; dc2; dc4]] [[F]] A Cascadia Mesh moderator offered in August to agree boundaries jointly; no follow-up appears in the record. [[@dc39; dc40]]

[[F]] Scoped packets already on the air carry psp, san and california, each matched on three or more packets. [[U]] One more packet matches us; that rests on a single match and is probable, not certain. [[@own-decode]] [[I]] That is two names from the airport family and at least one from the generator's chain. [[I]] Because matching is per name, a repeater set up from one list and a radio set up from another can meet only on a name both lists contain. Working drafts 3 to 5, the generator and the wiki draft all contain socal, and the generator and the wiki draft both contain us. No metro or county name appears in more than one of those three lists. [[F]] Members fill in some of what is on the air: the psp region was created by the Coachella Valley area's administrator, and repeaters in Rancho Mirage carry SoCal and PSP; a Lake Elsinore operator runs an le scope and on 12 October proposed rsc for Riverside County; a member answered that there are "too many regions in the plans. OC does not need 5". [[@dc44; dc45; dc17]] [[I]] A fifth vocabulary is forming on its own. That is the strongest argument for publishing the county list soon, and the strongest argument against this plan adding names of its own.

[[R]] This plan follows the maintainers. The names are the generator's: socal as the wide name, the county names below it, and the local areas as optional tags where a community wants one, all under the root chain the administrator and the generator's maintainer have settled (us, west, california). The airport-code metros of working drafts 3 to 5 are withdrawn. [[@dc9; dc12; mk-data:L112-118, L945-1326]] [[I]] The earlier worry that a Conejo repeater would carry six names is softened by region def, which sets the whole chain in one line on 1.16, and by the fact that a name costs nothing on the air unless a packet is scoped to it. [[@dc24; fw-packet:L52-58]]

[[O]] Two things from the withdrawn options are worth putting to the maintainers as questions, not proposals. First, MeshMapper's zones use airport codes, so zone names and region names will differ; that is harmless as long as the Wardriving Scope setting carries the region name. Second, duplicate low-level names across regions (a southbay in SoCal and one in the Bay) were judged acceptable in the thread because repeaters in between do not carry them; the same logic says a short local tag can be reused far away without harm. [[@dc12; dc16]]

Naming rules, whichever set is chosen

  • [[F]] Lower case, at most 29 bytes, letters, digits and hyphen. [[@blog-region]]
  • [[R]] No name should be the beginning of another name on the same repeater unless the shorter one is always present. [[F]] The CLI's allowf, denyf, get and default match by prefix when no exact name exists. [[@fw-regionmap:L218-231]] [[C]] Checked against the generator's SoCal list: west is the beginning of westside and westernriverside, and imperial of imperialvalley and imperialborder. [[@mk-data:L112-118, L945-1326]] [[I]] Harmless on a repeater that holds the full chain, because the exact name wins; a repeater tagged with only a local area and not its parents could pick the wrong name from a typed command. Worth a line in the generator's guidance, and a question for its maintainer rather than a change.
  • [[R]] A published name is never renamed. [[F]] "any misspellings will be treated as a completely different region". [[@blog-region]]
  • [[R]] The tree is the generator's: each county under socal, each area under its county, and the root chain above. [[F]] Nesting does not change forwarding, so a flat set would relay the same packets; the tree is kept so that every repeater's region listing has the same shape and the generator's lines paste without edits. [[@pnw-rollout:L105; mk-app:L923-935]]

Repeater roles

  • [[R]] County repeater. Holds its county name and socal (with the root chain above). Most repeaters, including peaks that serve one county.
  • [[R]] Wide-only site. Holds socal and the root chain only. For a site whose measured job is to link counties, or the Bear Mountain bridge near Tehachapi, where SoCal's frequency meets the Central Valley's.
  • [[R]] Overlap repeater. Holds two county names and socal. [[I]] It gives people in the seam coverage under either county's scope. It retransmits each county's floods, so they are heard one hop into the other county, and they stop there because a packet has one code and the next repeater along holds only its own county's name. A chain of overlap repeaters would carry them further, so keep such sites few. [[@fw-rep:L997-1005; blog-region]] [[F]] The coordination thread already treats Santa Maria as a border area that could carry both San Luis Obispo and Santa Barbara. [[@dc-memo]]
  • [[R]] Local tag. One of the generator's area names, held by the repeaters that serve that community, for a place that sits across a county line or wants its own channel scope. [[F]] The PNW plan uses the same device, and one survey answer was "Counties seem good, plus certain tags". [[@pnw-rollout:L113; dc8]]

07 · Answer to the Q2 report

What the State of the Mesh already said about regions

[[F]] The Q2 report says "Since we are on our own frequency, we pretty much have a built in region setting", prefers "to keep things local and not implement bridges", and notes that "There still is no way of decoding scopes in the companion software so everyone will still see every message, even if it is scoped." [[@wc-q2:L78-80]] This plan accepts all three points.

  1. [[I]] Frequency gives SoCal most of its boundary, not all of it. [[F]] A bridge between SoCal's 927.875 and the Central Valley's 910.525 has run on Bear Mountain near Tehachapi since at least March; its operator set it to block unscoped traffic on 10 August to stop ducted Bay Area traffic; and internet bridging has pushed Idaho and Washington traffic into SoCal. [[@dc42; dc36; dc37; dc49]] [[F]] The SoCal administrator's position on 12 October: "Keeping traffic local is priority so bridging has been pushed to the side until it is smart". [[@dc5]] [[I]] So socal and the root chain above it already have a job at the northern edge, where the Central Valley bridge sits. This plan proposes no new bridges.
  2. [[I]] The proposal is about floods inside SoCal. A wide socal scope changes nothing about who relays a message inside an isolated mesh, and it adds 4 bytes to each flood. [[@fw-packet:L52-58]] The saving comes only from scopes smaller than the whole mesh, applied to the traffic that is large.
  3. [[F]] Scoping changes who relays a message. It hides nothing. Companions do not filter on receive. [[@wc-q2:L80; blog-region]]
  4. [[I]] Scoping would not stop the tunneling the report describes. [[F]] The report describes someone "taking Bay Area traffic and injecting it into PNW". [[@wc-q2:L84]] [[F]] A region's key is derived from its public name and a packet's code from the packet's own bytes, and private regions have no working key storage. [[@fw-tks:L4-18, L44-47, L52-76]] [[I]] So anyone who knows a region's name can stamp a valid code on injected traffic. Strict forwarding would drop injected traffic that carries no code, and nothing more. [[F]] The long-time operator's gotchas list makes the same point: scoping is not security, because the codes derive from the public name. [[@dc13]]

[[F]] The report presents itself as one view: it is "not a definitive stance of the mesh". [[@wc-q2:L12]] [[F]] Its suggestion to limit bots to acknowledgements is worded as a proposal, ending "Can discuss further." [[@wc-q2:L39]]

08 · Rollout

Six steps, each with a test that can fail

[[O]] The thresholds below are starting proposals. [[I]] No community this plan looked at has published results for scoping inside a single isolated mesh. Scotland denied unscoped floods first, under pressure from a neighbor on its frequency. The Netherlands makes strict forwarding a required last step. Canada's proposal is experimental. The PNW plan has no dates. [[@scot-regions:L15, L66-74; nl-guide:L42, L157-164; ca-onqc:L8-12; pnw-rollout:L5-10]]

  1. 1
    Now: settings that need no agreement. [[R]] Apply the WCMesh repeater settings as published. Ask bot owners to consider acknowledgement-only behavior, as the Q2 report suggests. Check that get flood.max.advert returns 8 or less on 1.16 and newer. Design a hop-limit trial. [[@wc-radio:L18-28; wc-q2:L39; fw-rep:L907]] [[F]] The bot ask already has an owner and wording: the SoCal administrator asked in June that bots on #test be limited, that meshbud be the default responder, and that other bots use a prefix or a unique channel; meshcore-bot has per-user and per-channel rate limits and a maximum response hop count; Sacramento's draft bot policy proposes test-only responses on Public, at most three test bots and a random reply delay. [[@dc33; dc34; dc35]]TestAirtime at reference repeaters, before and after.
  2. 2
    Decide and measure. [[R]] The county-level names confirmed by the SoCal administrator, in the State of the Mesh or the coordination thread. A firmware census. Baseline counters from at least five repeaters in different counties. The names loaded into the analyzer and into MeshMapper, which "has to know a scope's name first". [[@cs-src-cfg:L400-404; dc50]]GateAll four exist in writing.
  3. 3
    Tags only. [[R]] Repeater owners add the county name and socal (the full chain, on 1.16) with flooding allowed. No default scope. The wildcard stays allowed. [[F]] An operator in the thread put it the same way: "rolling out regions as advisory with flood enabled for a turn or two. Prematurely turning off flood would be a disaster". [[@dc26]]Gate, per county[[O]] A message scoped to the county, sent from one edge, is heard at every observer in that county on three separate days.
  4. 4
    Default scope on repeaters. [[R]] region default socal first, on repeaters whose neighbors all hold it; a county default only where the whole county passed the gate and the administrator agrees. [[F]] His guidance in July: set default, leave home unset, and make the default a higher-level region such as SoCal rather than a local one.   5. 5
    Machines, then people. [[R]] The MeshMapper administrator sets the Wardriving Scope for that zone, and bot owners set the bot radio's default scope to its county. [[@mm-admin; blog-default]] [[I]] By all signs the SoCal administrator also administers MeshMapper's SoCal zones ("i made LAX cover a lot", answering a request to add PSP), so steps 2 and 5 have the same owner. [[@dc41]] Later, people may scope local channels, and companion default scope moves to socal. Emergency and alert channels stay unscoped until the gate for people is passed, then move to socal so that step 6 cannot cut them off.Gates[[O]] For machines: MeshMapper coverage unchanged week over week and relays per flood down. For people: a socal-scoped message reaches every observer site and at least 95 percent of repeaters hold socal.
  6. 6
    Containment, optional. [[R]] On 1.16 and newer, step flood.max.unscoped down from 12, holding each value for 30 days. Consider region denyf * only by agreement across the whole mesh. Region commands

[[F]] Five rules first. (1) Names are lower case, at most 29 bytes, letters, digits and hyphen. [[@blog-region]] (2) Type full names and read the echo, because get, allowf, denyf, default and the parent of put match by prefix when no exact name exists; the generator's list already has west before westside and imperial before imperialvalley. [[@fw-regionmap:L218-231; mk-data:L112-118, L945-1326]] (3) Before any allowf, denyf or default, run region get <name> and check that the reply echoes exactly the name you meant. [[@fw-cli:L1086-1097]] (4) region default saves everything at once and invents a region from a typo. [[@fw-cli:L1109-1128]] (5) [[R]] Work over USB or from direct radio range, and confirm you can still log in before you leave.

0. Inventory, any version. Read the firmware generation from the first line.
ver
region
region default
get flood.max
get flood.max.advert
get flood.max.unscoped
get loop.detect
get advert.interval

[[F]] On firmware older than 1.16 the two longer get flood.max.* lines are answered with the plain flood.max value, 64 by default, because the older code matches only the first nine letters. They mean something only on 1.16 and newer, so go by ver. [[@fw-v115:L782-783]]

1. Tags only, firmware 1.16 or newer. County repeater in Ventura County, with the full chain.
region def us west california socal ventura
region save
region get ventura
region get socal

[[F]] This is the line the generator emits. Each name in a region def line becomes a child of the one before it, so the five names form one tree, exactly as the generator builds it; name|jump moves back up the tree to start a branch. The command answers with the resulting tree. Each region get should answer with the name, its parent in parentheses, then F, meaning flooding is allowed: ventura (socal) F. [[@mk-app:L923-935; fw-cli:L1012-1057, L1086-1097]] [[F]] A line of the form us|* west|* ..., which the first pass of this revision showed, would instead place every name directly under the root as a flat set. Forwarding would be the same, but the tree would not match other repeaters and region get would print it differently.

2. Tags only, firmware 1.15.
region put us
region put west us
region put california west
region put socal california
region put ventura socal
region save
region get ventura
region get socal

[[F]] The second word after put is the parent, so this builds the same tree as block 1, one name at a time, which is also what the generator emits for 1.15. Each region put answers "OK - (flood allowed)". [[@fw-v115:L971-981; mk-app:L1036-1052]]

3. Tags only, firmware 1.12 to 1.14. Upgrade instead if the site can be reached.
region put us
region allowf us
region put west us
region allowf west
region put california west
region allowf california
region put socal california
region allowf socal
region put ventura socal
region allowf ventura
region save

[[F]] On these versions a new region starts denied, and there is no default scope. [[@fw-v1141:L1234-1245; blog-default]] [[F]] Version 1.11 only matches names that begin with #; the requirement was dropped in 1.12. [[@fw-v111:L123-134; blog-region]]

4. Other roles, firmware 1.16 or newer. Run one line, then region save.
region def us west california socal
region def us west california socal ventura|socal santabarbara
region put conejovalley ventura

The first line is a wide-only backbone site. The second is an overlap repeater on the Ventura and Santa Barbara seam: ventura|socal places ventura, then moves back up to socal so that santabarbara lands beside it rather than under it. The third adds a local tag under its county, which is how the generator places tags on every firmware. On 1.15 and older, add the names one region put name parent at a time as in blocks 2 and 3. [[@mk-app:L923-935, L1026-1033]]

5. Default scope, rollout step 4 only, firmware 1.15 or newer.
region get socal
region default socal
advert

[[F]] The reply must read "default scope is now socal". It saves immediately. advert sends a flood advert at once so you can check it still reaches an observer. Undo with region default <null>. [[@fw-cli:L195-197, L1109-1128]]

6. Rollout step 6, option A: limit unscoped hops. Firmware 1.16 or newer. Never combine with block 7.
set flood.max.unscoped 12
get flood.max.unscoped

[[F]] Saved on entry; no region save needed. Undo with set flood.max.unscoped 64. A value of 0 has the same effect as denying the wildcard. [[@fw-cli:L632-639; fw-routing:L19; ca-onqc:L617]]

7. Rollout step 6, option B: strict forwarding. Firmware 1.15 or newer, by mesh-wide agreement only.
region denyf *
region save

[[F]] Undo with region allowf * then region save. [[@pnw-rollout:L241]]

8. Remove a name added by mistake. The name must be exact and must have no children.
region remove ventura-typo
region save

11 · Pilots

Two pilots, because they answer different questions

Pilot A, mechanics, Conejo Valley.

  1. Partners. At least three contiguous repeaters: SkyNet, one neighbor toward Los Angeles, and NP-CAM Grade, which carried SkyNet's advert to the Camarillo observer in one hop. [[@cs-advert]] Ask the analyzer's maintainer to load the test names first.
  2. Instrument. On-node counters on each pilot repeater, read before and after: flood packets received and sent, duplicates, receive errors, transmit and receive airtime. [[@fw-stats:L28-50; fw-rep:L232-233; own-telemetry]] The analyzer is the secondary check.
  3. Configure. Tags only, using the block for each node's firmware. No default scope yet.
  4. Test 1, positive control. Send a message scoped to the pilot tag from one end of the cell. Pass: a companion at the other end, two hops away, receives it.
  5. Test 2, negative control. The same message must not be received by a companion one hop outside the cell, and the analyzer must show no path that leaves the pilot nodes. If it leaks: suspect a pre-1.10 repeater and record which one. [[@blog-region]]
  6. Test 3, unscoped control. An unscoped message sent in the same minute still arrives outside the cell.
  7. Test 4, the app and capitals. Type the scope in mixed case in the app and repeat Test 1. This is the case test that matters.
  8. Test 5, default scope. Set the default on one pilot node, send advert, and count the observations the analyzer records against the 64 it recorded for SkyNet's unscoped advert on 11 October. [[@cs-advert]] Prediction: far fewer, possibly none.
  9. Test 6, direct messages. With one companion's default scope set to the pilot tag and the other unset, exchange direct messages both ways and note which acknowledgements arrive.

[[I]] Pilot A can prove the mechanism and expose leaks. It cannot show a load reduction, because a few test messages are invisible against roughly 6,600 floods a day. [[@cs-retx]]

Pilot B, load, one MeshMapper zone. [[R]] Pick the zone with the best repeater coverage of its tag, set the Wardriving Scope for that zone only, and compare relays per #wardriving ping and MeshMapper coverage results for two weeks before and after. [[@mm-admin; cs-retx]] This is the pilot that can show the benefit, and it needs the MeshMapper administrator.

Appendix

Reading the scopes already on air

[[F]] The firmware derives a region's key as the first 16 bytes of SHA-256 over # plus the name, and a packet's code as the first two bytes of HMAC-SHA256 under that key over one byte of payload type followed by the payload, with the values 0x0000 and 0xFFFF moved to their neighbors. [[@fw-regionmap:L175-183; fw-tks:L4-18, L44-47]] [[F]] The analyzer serves the raw bytes of scoped packets and marks them as scoped with no resolved name. [[@cs-packets; cs-src-db:L3270-3278]] [[F]] Of 13 such packets from 11 to 13 October, 12 match a name: three flood adverts from one Coachella Valley repeater match psp; three Public messages from one user match san; four posts from a bot match california; two messages from another user match us and california. [[@own-decode]] [[F]] Twelve of the 13 came from the analyzer's latest 58 transport-route packets and one was fetched separately. The lone us match is therefore probable, not certain.

Python 3, standard library only. Returns the code a packet would carry under a given name.
import hashlib, hmac

def code(name, raw_hex):
    b = bytes.fromhex(raw_hex)
    ptype = (b[0] >> 2) & 0x0F               # payload type from the header byte
    n = b[5]                                 # path length byte, after two 16-bit codes
    path = ((n >> 6) + 1) * (n & 0x3F)       # hash size times hop count
    payload = b[6 + path:]
    key = hashlib.sha256(('#' + name).encode()).digest()[:16]
    mac = hmac.new(key, bytes([ptype]) + payload, hashlib.sha256).digest()
    return mac[:2].hex().upper()             # compare with raw_hex[2:6]
